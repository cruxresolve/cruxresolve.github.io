What It Is

GhostBridge is a transparent WiFi-to-RS232 bridge. It doesn't process, interpret, or interfere with the data passing through it — it just moves bytes between a WiFi TCP connection and a serial port, in both directions, as fast as the hardware allows.

On one end: a DB9 RS232 connector that plugs into the diagnostic serial port on your device. On the other: a WiFi access point your phone connects to directly. Power comes from USB-C. That's the whole thing.

How It's Built

The bridge is built around an ESP32 microcontroller paired with a MAX3232 RS232 level shifter. The ESP32 handles the WiFi stack and TCP server; the MAX3232 handles the RS232 voltage conversion so the signal levels are correct for the device on the other end.

The enclosure is a custom design we've iterated through twelve revisions to get right. Rev 12 features reinforced connector closures, M3 screw retention on both ends, anti-flex ribs, rubber feet, and a debossed ghost logo on the lid. It's printed in PLA and assembled by hand before shipping.

Each unit runs firmware that creates its own WiFi access point with a unique network name derived from its MAC address. No router required — your phone connects directly to the bridge. The TCP server listens on port 35000 at 192.168.4.1.

What It Works With

GhostBridge is designed as the hardware companion to GhostTune, our iOS EFI tuning application. But the bridge itself doesn't know or care what application is on the other end of the TCP connection, or what device is on the other end of the DB9 cable.

Any application that can open a TCP socket and send serial data can use GhostBridge. Any RS232 device — engine control units, industrial PLCs, legacy test equipment, data loggers, embedded systems — can be bridged wirelessly with it. The bridge is dumb by design. That's what makes it flexible.
